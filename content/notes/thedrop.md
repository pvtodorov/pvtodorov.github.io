# Ripples I'm looking forward to at The Drop 2026

I'm heading to the Drop this year and looking forward to connecting with founders, investors, and other key players in the deeptech ecosystem who focus on planetary health and resilience. Here are some of the roundtables and side-events I found to be interesting (will be a difficult choice to pick which ones to attend!)

Looking forward to run into and speak with others who share the same interests.

- **[Natural Solutions: Valuable or Money Wasted?](https://luma.com/m6jayd88)**, Ripple Area 4, hosted by [Jeff Johnson](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jeffreyjohnson/) ([B Capital](https://www.linkedin.com/company/b-capital-group)) and [Kyle Teamey](https://www.linkedin.com/in/kyle-teamey-9a53197) ([RA Capital Planetary Health](https://www.linkedin.com/company/ra-capital-management-llc)). On whether natural climate solutions are a sound use of limited climate funding. The page notes marginal abatement cost studies indicating emissions can be abated more efficiently through building insulation and vehicle emissions programmes, and asks why there are so few successful businesses leveraging natural solutions and whether they should sit with philanthropies and governments instead. For people currently investing, or considering investing, time or money in natural solutions.

- **[Hardware & Biology: Durable Moats Beyond Code](https://luma.com/gc1tdw4k)**, Ripple Area 7, hosted by Max Lebeau ([Positron Ventures](https://www.linkedin.com/company/positron-ventures)) and [Isabel Zhang](https://www.linkedin.com/in/isabel-zhang/) ([HCVC](https://www.linkedin.com/company/hardware-club)). On structural defensibility in hardware and biology. The page's thesis: "As AI commoditizes software features overnight, hardware and biological systems offer structural defensibility that code cannot match." It covers how founders and investors build lasting advantage while navigating capital intensity, regulatory complexity and physical constraints, and argues the next decade of defensibility lives in atoms and cells rather than algorithms. For hardware and biotech founders, software people looking at deeptech, and investors refining their thesis.

- **[Climate Risk & the Insurability Crisis](https://luma.com/enpf9hbi)**, Ripple Area 8, hosted by [Laura Fox](https://www.linkedin.com/in/lauraafox/) ([Streetlife Ventures](https://www.linkedin.com/company/streetlifevc)) and [Michael Langguth](https://www.linkedin.com/in/langguthmichael/) ([Carbon13](https://uk.linkedin.com/company/carbon13)). On the gap between climate losses and insurance coverage. Figures cited: over $12 trillion of US home value projected at risk over the next thirty years, over €800 billion in European climate-related losses since 1980, only about 25% of European losses covered, and nearly 40 million American homes facing reduced insurance availability. Questions cover which sectors face the most pressing insurability challenges, which approaches are economically viable and scalable, and what partnerships are needed to deploy them. Stated tension: significant changes to the built environment versus unlivable areas. For investors, founders, corporates and family offices holding real estate, energy and transport assets, plus insurance professionals.

- **[What Nature Markets Need to Scale](https://luma.com/wttd4uva)**, Ripple Area 6, hosted by [Ed Thorne](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ethorne1/) ([Sand River](https://uk.linkedin.com/company/sand-river)) with Kevin Webb. On the enabling technologies and market infrastructure nature markets need in order to scale. Their framing: "The clean energy transition didn't happen because of wind farms", it happened "because of batteries, turbines and PV cells." Questions cover which bottlenecks are blocking institutional capital and who is tackling them first, whether regenerative technologies can outcompete extractive ones on economics alone, and what nature markets infrastructure looks like by 2030. For VCs, LPs, founders, students and journalists covering nature tech.

- **[Conventional agriculture: the efficiency myth](https://luma.com/lx7iowcj)**, Ripple Area 10, hosted by [Sanne Bootsma](https://nl.linkedin.com/in/sanne-bootsma) ([DOEN Ventures](https://www.linkedin.com/company/doen-participaties/)) and [Erica van Eeghen](https://nl.linkedin.com/in/ericavaneeghen) ([VP Capital](https://www.linkedin.com/company/vpcapital-familyoffice)). On whether regenerative agriculture can become the economically superior model, and what it would take to realign finance, policy, technology and incentives around it. Questions cover whether healthcare and insurance should help fund food system resilience, technology's role in nature-aligned farming, how costs are shared across beneficiaries, and the mismatch between soil regeneration cycles of 7 to 10 years and capital cycles of 3 to 5 years. Stated tension: short-term economic efficiency versus long-term ecosystem resilience. For investors, agtech founders, corporate sustainability leads, farmers, policymakers and financial institutions.

- **[European Sovereignty Playbook](https://luma.com/bg7zzwf9)**, Ripple Area 5, hosted by [Maxi Pethö-Schramm](https://de.linkedin.com/in/anneke-maxi-peth%C3%B6-schramm) ([HV Capital](https://www.linkedin.com/company/hvcapital)) and [Birthe Ross](https://de.linkedin.com/in/birthe-ross/de) ([SPRIND](https://www.linkedin.com/company/sprind/)). On building "European primes" across energy, critical minerals, semiconductors, robotics, defence and compute. Questions cover the right capitalisation structures for companies destined to be critical national or continental assets, how to build government relationships and win contracts, and how to reach continental scale when sovereignty is defined and funded nationally. Stated tension: international capital versus domestic anchoring, European autonomy versus global competitiveness, and government process versus startup speed. For founders in dual-use, energy and industrial sectors, public sector operators, sovereign investors and policymakers.

- **[Unlocking Hidden Innovation](https://luma.com/wuvj8a8s)**, Ripple Area 8, hosted by [Andrea Siciliani](https://www.linkedin.com/in/andreasiciliani/) ([Techenergy Ventures](https://www.linkedin.com/company/techenergyventures)) and [Leonardo Massa](https://it.linkedin.com/in/leonardomassa) ([MITO Technology](https://www.linkedin.com/company/mito-technology)). On commercialising university and corporate R&D, which the hosts describe as "a goldmine of innovation that never reaches the market, buried under slow IP deals, misaligned incentives, and ecosystems that don't talk to each other." Figures cited, all from 2025: 76 university spin-outs crossed $1B valuations, VCs deployed over €8B into European deep tech, and corporates including Bosch and Tenova launched structured spin-out programmes. Questions cover university versus corporate spin-out patterns, which best practices work and why they have not spread, and whether the bigger unlock is fixing each side separately or connecting them into one ecosystem. For deep tech investors, corporate innovation leads, tech transfer officers and spin-out founders.

- **[Amid Market Backlash: Pitching Climate 2026](https://luma.com/kzx18ncg)**, Ripple Area 3, hosted by [Philip Specht](https://www.linkedin.com/in/philip-specht-b75972a3/) ([AENU](https://www.linkedin.com/company/aenu)) and [Hugo Rauch](https://www.linkedin.com/in/hugorauch/) (New Wave and [Climate Club](https://www.linkedin.com/company/climateclub-cc/)). On how climate is currently pitched across founders, VCs, LPs and corporates, and how the narrative may need to evolve in 2026. Context given: over the last two to three years political support has weakened in key markets, geopolitical tension has pushed climate priorities into the background, the AI wave is absorbing most of the tech ecosystem's attention, capital has become more selective, LP scrutiny has intensified, and many climate startups report longer sales cycles. Questions include whether the climate movement has a marketing problem and whether the sector should move beyond the term "climate tech". For founders pitching to corporates and VCs, VCs pitching to LPs, and corporate leads pitching internally.

- **[Can Robots secure Europe's Food System?](https://luma.com/8yda2rik)**, Ripple Area 6, hosted by [Julius Strauss](https://www.linkedin.com/in/julius-strauss/) ([FoodLabs](https://www.linkedin.com/company/foodlabsvc/)) and [Philip Stark](https://www.linkedin.com/in/philipstark/) ([Oyster Bay](https://www.linkedin.com/company/oysterbayvc/)). On agricultural robotics and European food security. The page opens: "Hormuz, Ukraine, AGI - threats to food supply are increasing, and Europe needs to make more of what we eat. But with labour costs rising and immigration rules tightening, can robots ride to the rescue?" Questions cover which opportunities are best (most urgent shortages, most robotics-ready), what makes robotics startups uniquely hard, and how Europe competes with China and the US. For founders and investors in ag robotics, and anyone interested in food security or dual-use defence applications.

- **[Food to Fashion to Food: Bio Closed-Loop](https://luma.com/nphtlr7p)**, Ripple Area 4, hosted by [Nic Gorini](https://www.linkedin.com/in/nicgorini/) ([Spin Ventures](https://uk.linkedin.com/company/spin-ventures)) and [Michelle de Rijk](https://www.linkedin.com/in/michelle-de-rijk-bba96661/) ([Fair Capital Impact Fund](https://www.linkedin.com/company/fair-capital-impact-fund/)). On closed-loop materials as an asset class, deploying biomaterials from food waste into fashion supply chains with recovery built in. Figures cited: the EU generates over 58 million tonnes of food waste annually, valued at about €132bn; the European textile and clothing sector comprises roughly 192,000 companies, 1.3 million workers and around €167bn in turnover; the European bioeconomy is valued at €2.7tn. Questions cover which biomaterial pathways can deliver venture-scale returns before 2030, how feedstock aggregation secures a cost advantage, and which infrastructure drives asset-level returns as textile collection volumes rise under 2025 EU rules. Stated tension: waste-to-value and insetting versus offsetting. For biotech and deeptech ventures, agri, consumer and retail operators, and LPs and GPs.

- **[De-risking the Dragon, embracing the Elephant](https://luma.com/8mf952hr)**, Ripple Area 6, hosted by [Divya Murthy](https://de.linkedin.com/in/divyabn) ([Oyster Bay](https://www.linkedin.com/company/oysterbayvc/)) and [Mark Kahn](https://ae.linkedin.com/in/mark-kahn-20490a) ([Omnivore](https://in.linkedin.com/company/omnivore-partners)). On European-Indian partnership in climate tech. The page argues China "might be the global leader in climate tech, but it will inevitably treat Europe as a vassal state", and positions India as an equal partner on the back of the recent EU-India trade treaty. Questions cover how European startups engage India as customer, supplier and research partner, why India is a better partnership candidate than China, and how to answer objections about India being "chaotic, corrupt, inefficient". For European startups and VC investors.

- **[Food investment - the next wave (the Tsunami)](https://luma.com/thedrop-buu0)**, Ripple Area 11, hosted by [Johan Jörgensen](https://www.linkedin.com/in/johanjorgensen/) ([Sweden Foodtech](https://www.linkedin.com/company/swedenfoodtech)) and [Björn Öste](https://www.linkedin.com/in/bj%C3%B6rn-%C3%B6ste-b185551/) (Good Idea). On new science in food and human nutrition and the investment opportunities that follow. The page states "Food will be bigger than AI - remember where you heard it first!" and frames food as "the major source of climate change, destroyed nature and bad human health". It names "the dry and flaming summer of -26" and the GLP-1 revolution as the events that drove the message home, and describes what is coming as a Copernican shift. For investors looking for new hypotheses, food tech investors and entrepreneurs.

- **[Infra-tech: New definitions & funding modes](https://luma.com/53sqyovx)**, Ripple Area 4, hosted by [Shakil Karim](https://www.linkedin.com/in/shakil-karim-5080b412a/) ([Actia Capital Partners](https://www.linkedin.com/company/actia-partners)) and [Toba Spiegel](https://at.linkedin.com/in/tobaspiegel) ([Loom Ventures](https://at.linkedin.com/company/loomvc)). On how infra tech has evolved, including blurring dividing lines between categories, and how business models and funding change with it. The urgency case given: "AI demand > supply > planetary boundaries with current infra." Questions cover practical definitions and lessons learned, what the emerging models look like, and to what extent new ways are appearing to support these companies capital-wise. For founders comparing scaling options, investors comparing notes, and incubators, government organisations and ecosystem players.

- **[Is Resilience a Lie We Tell Ourselves?](https://luma.com/070moc9e)**, Ripple Area 9, hosted by [Till Stenzel](https://www.linkedin.com/in/drtillstenzel/) ([SET Ventures](https://www.linkedin.com/company/setventures)) and [Katharina Beitz](https://www.linkedin.com/in/thedigitalkatharinabeitz/) ([Vireo Ventures](https://www.linkedin.com/company/vireo-ventures)). On cybersecurity in distributed energy systems. The page's framing: "Decentralization removes the honeypot but creates millions of entry points." It cites the planned cyberattack on distributed energy assets in Poland in late 2025, states that the gap between deployment speed and security readiness is "widening - not closing", and notes that most energy tech investors still do not look at cybersecurity when evaluating deals. It also asks where the line sits between civilian infrastructure and strategic assets once energy assets become targets of cyber warfare. For operators of distributed energy assets, energy tech investors and founders.

- **[Mine or Be Mined](https://luma.com/vjr3v0xh)**, Ripple Area 2, hosted by [Lasse Köhler](https://www.linkedin.com/in/lassekohler/) ([EIFO](https://www.linkedin.com/company/eifo)) and [Johan Attby](https://www.linkedin.com/in/johanattby/) ([Norrsken Evolve](https://www.linkedin.com/company/norrsken-evolve)). On Europe's strategic choice in critical minerals: strengthen domestic extraction and processing, deepen partnerships with trusted allies, or accelerate investment in technologies that reduce reliance on scarce materials. Set against recent export controls on rare earth elements and implementation of the EU Critical Raw Materials Act. The page notes extraction, processing and refining are "capital-intensive, complex, and subject to long development cycles, making them a challenging fit for traditional venture capital", and asks what role sovereign and philanthropic capital should play alongside VC. For climate and industrial tech investors, battery and advanced materials founders, corporates with supply chain exposure, and policymakers.

- **[Ocean tech: too early or mis-funded?](https://luma.com/5b5vg4tm)**, Ripple Area 5, hosted by [Jerome Ternynck](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jerometernynck/) ([Rypples](https://www.linkedin.com/company/rypples/)) and [James Lindsay](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jamesarthurlindsay/) ([Builders Vision](https://www.linkedin.com/company/builders-vision)). On why ocean ecosystems, described as "one of the biggest buffers we have", remain chronically underfunded. Questions cover whether ocean tech is genuinely too early for venture or has been measured with the wrong metrics, where the bottleneck sits between technology readiness, patient capital and investor familiarity, and which sub-sectors are closest to breakout. Stated tension: capital efficiency versus planetary necessity. The page notes this is a debate rather than an intro session, aimed at climate and deep tech investors plus those in adjacent verticals with indirect ocean exposure.

- **[What future will the boldest aspire to build?](https://luma.com/0cq5mh5f)**, Ripple Area 8, hosted by [Anna Ottosson](https://se.linkedin.com/in/annaottosson) ([Mudcake](https://se.linkedin.com/company/mudcake)) and [Jonny Everett](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jonnyeverett/) ([Marble](https://www.linkedin.com/company/marble-climate)). On directing ambitious founders and operators towards climate and planetary problems. The page asks how to ensure the smartest and boldest are not spending the coming decade "heads down building wealth without a purpose", drawn by "the lure of building AI startups without any intention of making the world better", and whether those technological leaps can be redirected towards "the problems really worth solving". For people looking to attract talent into building a meaningful future.

## Two side events, not Ripples

- **[The Edge of the Possible with ARIA: Pitching the future of climate tech](https://luma.com/vryqyj1b)**, Tuesday 10:00, hosted by the UK's [Advanced Research and Invention Agency](https://uk.linkedin.com/company/advanced-research-and-invention-agency) with [The Drop](https://www.linkedin.com/company/the-drop-conf/). The prompt is "£50 million. One shot. What overlooked research would you fund to change the world?" ARIA describes itself as an R&D funding agency built to do things differently, backing research that is under-explored but could deliver major societal and economic impact, from individuals and academia through to startups and industry. Format is two halves of 45 minutes, an ideas exchange with no panels, then networking. Their own description: "It's a pitch night, but not as you know it."

- **CVC Bridges @ The Drop 2026**, [event page here](https://luma.com/r4woru3l), Tuesday 14:00 to 18:00 at Ångbåtsbron 1, an official but independently run side event. Co-hosted by [Pale Blue Dot](https://www.linkedin.com/company/palebluedotvcclimate/), [Deepsense](https://www.linkedin.com/company/deepsensenetwork/) and [RosholmDell](https://www.linkedin.com/company/rosholmdell-advokatbyra/), with the Chamber of Commerce and Industry of Southern Sweden and Hello Tomorrow as community partners. The stated premise is that climate and deeptech founders operate on long-term cycles, so VCs and CVCs must evolve into collaborative stewards that help de-risk long-term growth. Opening remarks from [Anna Normann](https://www.linkedin.com/in/anna-normann-54267819/) and [Johan Eklund](https://www.linkedin.com/in/johan-eklund-a8a7b91/) of the Chamber of Commerce and [Heidi Lindvall](https://www.linkedin.com/in/heidilindvall/) of Pale Blue Dot. Then a fireside panel, "The sovereign capital stack: Funding European deep tech to bankability", with [Danijel Višević](https://www.linkedin.com/in/visevic/) (World Fund), [Iris ten Have](https://www.linkedin.com/in/iris-ten-have/) (Visionaries VC), [Ethan Sohn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ethan-m-sohn/) (Toyota Ventures) and [Julien Fredonie](https://www.linkedin.com/in/fredoniejulien/) (Honda Xcelerator Ventures), covering where EU deeptech stands and where CVCs and VCs can collaborate more effectively, gaps in partnership structures reflected in VC and CVC term sheets and board advisory, and real stories of founder-first governance. Four open ripples follow: securing access to the future of compute and semiconductor supply chain with [Bailey Morrow](https://www.linkedin.com/in/bailey-morrow-b763565/) (HSBC Innovation) and [Daniel I-Fan Wu](https://www.linkedin.com/in/danielifanwu/) (Hyundai Cradle); robots becoming reliable through operation rather than demonstration with [Isabel Zhang](https://www.linkedin.com/in/isabel-zhang/) (HCVC) and [Laura Coppen](https://www.linkedin.com/in/laura-coppen/) (H&M Ventures); storage earning like infrastructure but funded like software with [Elena Ballesteros](https://www.linkedin.com/in/elena-ballesteros-rodado/) (Voyager Ventures) and [Celeste Galvani](https://www.linkedin.com/in/celeste-galvani/) (A2A); and removing the bottlenecks with automating industry and infrastructure with [Philipp Buddemeier](https://www.linkedin.com/in/philipp-buddemeier/) (Marvelous VC) and [Finn Aretz](https://www.linkedin.com/in/finnaretz/) (Deepsense). It closes with a reciprocity circle and 90 minutes of networking.
